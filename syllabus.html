About the course

Art Lab: Foundations is a mixed-media fine arts course built around observation, experimentation, and a deeper understanding of materials and process. Students learn to see carefully, draw with confidence, and make choices that give their work meaning.

The semester begins with drawing, observation, color, and visual thinking. As students build confidence, the course expands into handmade paint, mixed media, layered surfaces, and selected sculptural and cementitious processes. Along the way, students learn how artists across history have used materials, from egg tempera and gold leaf to plaster and print, to shape what their work communicates. Materials are one of the ways students learn to think and work like artists, not the entire subject of the course.

The course closes with a more self-directed final project, planned, tested and refined over a full month, in which students choose the processes that best serve their own idea.

Art Lab: Foundations is designed primarily for high school students and is also an excellent fit for motivated middle school students. No advanced art experience is required; students build their skills as they go.

Sketchbook practice
5–10 MIN

4–6 days a week, every week of the course.

Short, frequent drawing keeps the observation skills from the first weeks growing all semester, even in weeks spent with paint and plaster. Each week has its own prompt, tied to what the class is learning.

The sketchbook also holds thumbnails, color recipes, pigment tests, material notes and mistakes worth remembering. It can be messy. It is evidence of thinking.

Example prompt, week 11Draw a shiny object, like a spoon, and map its reflections as shapes.
The bookend drawing

In Week 1 students draw an ordinary object. Before the final class they draw it again with the same setup. Side by side, the two drawings show a semester of growth more clearly than any grade.

Critique, online

Each week three students are spotlighted live, and everyone shares in the class gallery. We begin with observation before interpretation, and ask questions before suggesting fixes. The aim is specific, useful feedback, never competition.

Course arc

The semester begins with drawing and visual thinking, then builds toward color, mixed media, sculptural material and a self-directed final project. Each phase sends students out once to look at art in the world.

Final project / Weeks 13–16

A self-directed final project

Create one substantial artwork, or a small, tightly connected body of work, that combines at least two material or process families from the semester.

The materials should support the idea, not simply appear because they were used in class.

A technically perfect object with no intentional choices is not automatically stronger than a rougher work with real investigation. Craft matters, but the course values observation, experimentation, judgment and thoughtful revision together.

Studio practice

Sketchbook, critique and assessment

The Art Lab sketchbook
  • One sketchbook for the whole semester, used 4–6 days a week for 5–10 minutes of drawing.
  • Weekly prompts connect daily drawing to the week's material, so observation keeps growing.
  • It also holds thumbnails, proportion notes, value studies, color recipes and pigment tests.
  • Material observations and mistakes worth remembering belong here too.
  • Final-project planning and the closing reflection live in its last pages.
  • Pages can be messy. They are evidence of thinking, not portfolio pieces.
How we talk about work
"I notice…""This makes me think…"
  • Begin with observation before interpretation.
  • Be specific about visual evidence: edge, value, scale, color, texture, focal point, rhythm, material.
  • Ask questions before prescribing a fix. The goal is to help the artist see choices, not redesign the work.
  • Students may disagree about meaning. Good critique separates what is visible from what is interpreted.

Materials & supplies

Supplies in three stages

Supplies are staged so families aren't buying everything at once. Specialty items are selected, and where possible pre-portioned, by the instructor. A separate Materials Guide lists every item week by week.

Weeks 1–3
Drawing

Graphite pencils HB–6B, charcoal, kneaded eraser, blending stump, toned paper and the Art Lab sketchbook.

Weeks 4–7
Paint lab

Pigment pastes, prepared casein binder, palette, brushes, panels and illustration board, collage papers and glue.

Weeks 8–16
Surface + form

Modeling paste, plasticine, casting plaster, carving tools, sandpaper, gilding size and imitation leaf, gel medium, gel plate, brayer, sun-print paper, gloves and goggles.

From homeEggs, a few household objects, cardboard and found materials, a desk lamp, a phone for photos, and old kitchen utensils that can be retired to art use.
